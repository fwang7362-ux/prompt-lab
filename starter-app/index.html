How this works

Your app has four screens, matching GLIM's four boxes on your Week 2 page: hook → help them see the problem → one trust line → one next step. Type GLIM's content into the boxes below and watch the phone preview. Then pick a route.

Route A · Make our linkFill in the boxes, press Make our link, and share it. Nothing to install. Best if you want a working app fast.
Route B · Change it with an AI toolFill in the boxes, press Download our app file, then change the design or add features with an AI tool and publish your own link. More freedom, more to check.

Your work stays in this browser until you press Start over. One person types; to move to another computer, make the link and use Continue from a link there.

About your app

Every app shows a fixed line at the top: "Student-built app · research prototype · not an offer from any company." You can't remove it.

Screen 1 · Hook (GLIM box 1)

Meet the customer at their moment, in their words. Not "Welcome!"

Screen 2 · Help them see the problem (GLIM box 2)

2–3 things the customer can notice in their own space. They tap "Yes, this happens here" or "Not here." This is where they diagnose; you don't sell.

If the customer says "Not here" to everything, the app says honestly that this may not be a problem in their space. That's a valid result.

Optional · Clap echo test

The customer claps once and the phone shows how long the room "rings." They can try two or three spots and compare. Use it only if your pain point is about echo or speech being hard to follow.

Phones are rough measuring tools. The test shows only a comparison between tries on the same phone: no "good" or "bad" levels and no official numbers. Nothing is recorded or sent. Test it on an iPhone and an Android phone before the field test.

Screen 3 · One trust line (GLIM box 3)

One sentence that answers the barrier in section 6 of your Brief. Show honesty; don't claim it.

Screen 4 · One next step (GLIM box 4)

One button. When the customer taps it, the app shows your thank-you message. Nothing is collected or sent.

Check and share

The copy check looks for words that often mean a price, a service promise, or a claim that needs a row in your claims table. It can't catch everything; your team still reads every sentence.
